## Tier 1: Dominant by sheer number (cosmic/thermal backgrounds)

**1.  (ν + e⁻ → ν + e⁻)**

- Both CC (νₑ only) and NC (all flavors)
- Happens constantly throughout the universe: solar neutrinos, cosmic neutrino background (CνB), supernova neutrinos, reactor neutrinos
- Cross section ~10⁻⁴⁴ cm² (MeV scale), but neutrino fluxes are enormous
- **Most prevalent weak scattering process in the universe**

## Tier 2: Very common in specific environments

**5. Neutrino-nucleus resonance scattering (Δ resonance, etc.)**
- ν + N → ℓ + Δ → ℓ + N + π
- Intermediate energies (~0.3–2 GeV)
- Significant in atmospheric and accelerator neutrino beams

**6. Neutrino-electron CC scattering (ν̄ₑ + e⁻ → ν̄ₑ + e⁻ via W; νₑ + e⁻ → νₑ + e⁻ via W+Z)**
- Glashow resonance (ν̄ₑ + e⁻ → W⁻ → anything) at E_ν ≈ 6.3 PeV
- Rare except at extreme energies or in νₑ beams
- Solar νₑ e-scattering is measurable but subdominant to νe elastic NC

**7. Neutrino-nucleus elastic scattering (NC, incoherent)**
- Subdominant to CEνNS at low energies; relevant at higher recoil

**8. Inverse beta decay (ν̄ₑ + p → n + e⁺)**
- Technically a scattering/capture; dominant detection channel in liquid scintillator detectors (KamLAND, Daya Bay, JUNO)
- Very high rates in reactors and supernovae

**9. Neutrino-deuteron scattering (CC and NC)**
- ν + d → e + p + n (CC), ν + d → ν + p + n (NC)
- Key in SNO solar neutrino detection
- Moderate rates in solar/astrophysical environments

## Tier 3: Common in detectors/lab, rare naturally

**10. Parity-violating electron-nucleon scattering (e + N → e + N via Z⁰)**
- Møller (e⁻e⁻), Qweak (e-p), SLAC E158
- Extremely small cross section (~10⁻⁶ of EM), but high-luminosity beams make it measurable
- Negligible natural prevalence

**11. Neutrino-proton elastic scattering (ν + p → ν + p, NC)**
- Subdominant to CEνNS and ν-e scattering

**12. Neutrino-nucleus CC coherent pion production**
- ν + A → ℓ + π + A
- Low rates, relevant in accelerator experiments

**13. Muon capture (μ⁻ + p → n + ν_μ)**
- Weak "scattering"/capture; occurs in muonic atoms
- Moderate rates in matter with stopped muons

**14. Neutrino-antineutrino scattering (ν + ν̄ → ...)**
- Extremely rare in normal matter (needs neutrino backgrounds)
- Relevant in early universe, supernova neutrino spheres, CνB

## Tier 4: Rare / exotic / hypothetical

**15. Neutrino-neutrino scattering (ν + ν → ν + ν)**
- Via Z⁰; significant only in dense neutrino environments (supernova core, early universe)
- Cross section tiny

**16. W/Z-mediated electron-quark scattering at high Q²**
- Deep inelastic e-N scattering via Z exchange (parity-violating)
- Dominated by photon exchange; weak contribution ~10⁻⁵

**17. Neutrino scattering off dark matter / WIMPs**
- Hypothetical; if WIMPs have weak couplings
- Negligible unless DM density × cross section is large

**18. Right-handed W_R scattering**
- Hypothetical (left-right symmetric models)

**19. Leptoquark-mediated scattering**
- Hypothetical

**20. Neutrino-axion / neutrino-hidden-sector scattering**
- Hypothetical

**21. Neutron-antineutron oscillation-related scattering**
- Hypothetical

## Summary Ranking (natural prevalence, universe-wide)

| Rank | Process | Environment |
|------|---------|-------------|
| 1 | ν–e elastic (CC+NC) | Everywhere (CνB, solar, SN, reactors) |
| 2 | CEνNS (ν–N coherent) | Supernovae, stars, detectors |
| 3 | ν–N quasi-elastic CC | Supernovae, solar, reactors, beams |
| 4 | ν–N deep inelastic (CC+NC) | High-energy astrophysics, accelerators |
| 5 | ν–N resonance (Δ) | Atmospheric, accelerator |
| 6 | ν̄ₑ + p → n + e⁺ (IBD) | Reactors, supernovae |
| 7 | ν–d scattering | Solar (SNO), astrophysical |
| 8 | ν–e CC (ν̄ₑ Glashow) | Ultra-high-energy only |
| 9 | μ⁻ capture | Muonic matter |
| 10 | Parity-violating e–N | Lab only |
| 11 | ν–ν scattering | SN core, early universe |
| 12 | Exotic/hypothetical | — |

**Key caveat**: In a **detector context** (e.g., liquid scintillator), inverse beta decay and ν–e elastic dominate. In a **supernova core**, ν–N (CEνNS, QE, IBD) and ν–ν dominate. In the **early universe**, ν–e and ν–ν scattering set neutrino decoupling. In **accelerator beams**, DIS and resonance dominate. So "prevalence" is environment-dependent—the ranking above is a best-effort universe-wide average.
