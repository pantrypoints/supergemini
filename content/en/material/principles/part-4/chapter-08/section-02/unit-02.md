- **IMFs involved:** Hydrogen bonding, ion–dipole, electrostatic
- **Why common:** Every acid–base reaction in water or protic solvent involves proton transfer through a **hydrogen-bonded network** (Grotthuss mechanism). The solvation of H⁺ and OH⁻ by ion–dipole and H-bonding is essential.

- **Examples:**
  - HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O (neutralization in water)
  - NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻
  - Carboxylic acid + amine → ammonium carboxylate salt
  - Enzyme active-site proton relays (e.g., serine proteases)

- **Role of IMFs:** H-bonding aligns the proton donor/acceptor; ion–dipole solvation stabilizes ions; the solvent's H-bond network mediates proton mobility.
