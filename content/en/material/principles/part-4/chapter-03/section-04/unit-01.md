The Intro-Aetherspace facilitates the following phenomena:

## 1. Radioactive Decay
- **Beta decay (β⁻)**: A neutron converts to a proton, emitting an electron and an electron antineutrino (n → p + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ). Mediated by the W⁻ boson.
- **Positron emission (β⁺)**: A proton converts to a neutron, emitting a positron and an electron neutrino (p → n + e⁺ + νₑ). Mediated by the W⁺ boson.
- **Electron capture**: A proton captures an orbital electron to become a neutron, emitting a neutrino (p + e⁻ → n + νₑ).
- **Inverse beta decay**: An antineutrino interacts with a proton to produce a neutron and positron (ν̄ₑ + p → n + e⁺). Important in neutrino detection.

## 2. Neutrino Interactions
- **Neutrino scattering** off electrons and nucleons (both charged-current and neutral-current interactions).
- **Neutrino oscillation**: While the oscillation itself is a quantum mechanical effect, the weak force is the only force neutrinos feel (besides gravity), so all neutrino detection and interaction phenomena are weak processes.
- **Solar neutrino capture** (e.g., Cl-37 + νₑ → Ar-37 + e⁻ in the Homestake experiment).
- **Coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering (CEνNS)**.

## 3. Quark Flavor-Changing Processes
- **Strange particle decays**: e.g., Λ → p + π⁻, K⁺ → π⁺ + π⁰ (strangeness-changing weak decays).
- **Charm and bottom quark decays**: e.g., D⁰ → K⁻ + π⁺, B⁰ → J/ψ + K⁰.
- **Top quark decay**: t → b + W⁺ (the top quark decays almost exclusively via the weak force).
- **CP violation** in neutral kaon and B-meson systems (observed in weak decays).

## 4. Nuclear Fusion in Stars
- **Proton-proton (pp) chain**: The first step, p + p → deuteron + e⁺ + νₑ, is a weak process (converts a proton to a neutron).
- **CNO cycle**: Involves beta decays (e.g., ¹³N → ¹³C + e⁺ + νₑ).
- **Solar hydrogen burning** overall depends on the weak force to convert protons into neutrons.

## 5. Big Bang Nucleosynthesis
- **Neutron-proton interconversion**: n + νₑ ↔ p + e⁻ and p + ν̄ₑ ↔ n + e⁺ during the early universe.
- **Free neutron decay** (n → p + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ) with a lifetime of ~880 s.
- **Deuterium formation**: p + n → D + γ, where the available neutron abundance is set by weak interactions.

## 6. Supernova Processes
- **Neutronization burst**: p + e⁻ → n + νₑ during core collapse.
- **Neutrino heating and cooling** in core-collapse supernovae (weak interactions drive the explosion mechanism).
- **r-process nucleosynthesis**: Neutron-rich environments shaped by weak interactions (beta decays, neutrino captures).

## 7. Higgs Boson Decays
- **H → W⁺W⁻ and H → ZZ** (weak gauge boson couplings).
- **H → fermion pairs** (e.g., H → τ⁺τ⁻, H → b b̄) — the Higgs couples to fermions with strength proportional to mass, and these decays involve weak isospin structure.

## 8. Electroweak Phenomena
- **W and Z boson production and decay** (e.g., at the LHC: q q̄' → W → ℓν).
- **Neutral current interactions**: Z boson exchange (e.g., e⁺e⁻ → Z → f f̄).
- **Parity violation in electron scattering** (e.g., SLAC E122, Q-weak experiment) — interference between photon and Z exchange.
- **Atomic parity violation** (e.g., in cesium).
- **Møller scattering asymmetry** (e⁻e⁻ → e⁻e⁻) due to Z exchange.

## 9. Muon and Tau Decays
- **Muon decay**: μ⁻ → e⁻ + ν̄ₑ + ν_μ (pure weak process via W⁻).
- **Tau decay**: τ⁻ → ν_τ + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ (or μ⁻ + ν̄_μ, or hadrons).
- **Pion decay**: π⁺ → μ⁺ + ν_μ and π⁺ → e⁺ + νₑ (weak annihilation of quarks).
- **Kaon decays** (e.g., K⁺ → μ⁺ + ν_μ).

## 10. Hadronic Weak Decays
- **Hyperon decays**: Σ⁻ → n + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ, Ξ⁻ → Λ + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ, etc.
- **Charmed and beauty baryon decays**.

## 11. Neutron Lifetime and Ultracold Neutron Experiments
- **Free neutron beta decay** — a pure weak process used to measure the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix element |V_ud|.

## 12. Weak Mixing and CKM Matrix Phenomena
- **Quark mixing** (Cabibbo angle, CKM matrix) — manifests in weak decays of hadrons.
- **Lepton mixing** (PMNS matrix) — manifests in neutrino oscillations and weak decays of leptons.

## 13. Dark Matter and Beyond-Standard-Model (hypothetical/possible)
- **Weakly Interacting Massive Particles (WIMPs)** — hypothetical dark matter candidates that would interact via the weak force (and gravity).
- **Neutrinoless double beta decay** (if observed, would involve Majorana neutrinos and weak interactions).

## 14. Precision Electroweak Tests
- **Z-pole observables** at LEP (e⁺e⁻ → Z → f f̄).
- **W mass and width measurements**.
- **Atomic parity violation** and **Q-weak** (already listed).
- **Neutrino-electron scattering** (e.g., reactor antineutrino experiments).
