### 4. Supramolecular Host–Guest Complexation and Self-Assembly (Cohesion)

- **IMFs involved:** Hydrophobic, hydrogen bonding, π–π stacking, cation–π, van der Waals
- **Why common:** Supramolecular chemistry is defined by IMFs. Host–guest binding, self-assembly, and molecular recognition are ubiquitous in chemistry, biology, and materials science.
- **Examples:**
  - Cyclodextrin/drug inclusion complexes
  - Cucurbituril–guest binding
  - DNA base pairing (H-bonding + π-stacking)
  - Protein–ligand binding (hydrophobic + H-bonding + π–π)
  - Micelle and bilayer formation (hydrophobic)
  - Metal–organic framework (MOF) assembly (coordination + π–π + H-bonding)
  - Rotaxane/catenane template synthesis
- **Role of IMFs:** These reactions **are** IMF-driven—binding, recognition, and assembly are entirely noncovalent.
