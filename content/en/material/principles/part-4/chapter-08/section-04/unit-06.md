## D. Notes on "Prevalence"

- **Free neutron beta decay** and **nuclear β decay** dominate cosmic prevalence because neutrons and radioactive nuclei are ubiquitous (stars, reactors, Earth's crust, early universe).
- **Muon and pion decays** dominate experimental event rates because cosmic-ray showers and accelerator beams produce them in enormous numbers.
- **Kaon, tau, charm, bottom, W/Z, top, Higgs** decays are prevalent **in colliders** but rare in nature (except cosmic-ray contributions).
- **Double beta decay** is extremely rare (τ ~10²¹ yr) but is a major topic in neutrino physics.
- **Proton decay** is hypothetical and not observed; included only for completeness.

---

**Bottom line:** By cosmic prevalence, **neutron beta decay and nuclear beta decay** lead. By experimental event rate, **muon and pion decays** lead. By combined major-weak-decay prevalence, the top tier is **neutron β decay, nuclear β decay, muon decay, and pion decay**.
