## Snell's Law by Descartes

Descartes independently derived **Snell's law** by thinking of light as quantized balls:

His key move was that the light speed and direction was affected by the **media** that light went through. This led to the principle of least action.

## Legacy and Limits

Descartes' optics achieved two lasting things: it gave a **clear mechanical rationale for the laws of reflection and refraction**, and it helped establish the **sine law** as the central quantitative principle of refraction. Yet its central assumption — that light travels faster in denser media — was wrong, and his instantaneous "pressure" model could not account for interference, diffraction, or the finite speed of light. Newton's corpuscular theory and later Huygens' and Young's wave optics would correct and supersede it.

Still, Descartes' account of the bending and bouncing of light remains a landmark: an ambitious attempt to reduce the behavior of light to the same mechanical principles that governed colliding bodies — and a vivid illustration of how a theory can get the *mathematics* right while getting the *physics* wrong. -->
