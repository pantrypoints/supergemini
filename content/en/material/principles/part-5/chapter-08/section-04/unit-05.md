**Shear Force** is the rotational, sliding force that arises when parallel internal layers of a material slide past one another, causing angular distortion without changing volume. It is the **Mid Cohesion force** in Superphysics' Solid Relationalities — the rotational, structure-forming action at the intermediate scale between the aetherspace and space.

Unlike tensile/compression (linear/displacement), shear force is **rotational and distortive** — it twists layers sideways, creating angular deformation that binds the material into a coherent sheared structure.

## The Equation: Shear Fundamentals

### Shear Stress (Force per Area)

$$
\tau = \frac{F}{A}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( \tau \) | Shear stress | \( \text{Pa} = \text{N}/\text{m}^2 \) |
| \( F \) | Applied tangential force | \( \text{N} \) |
| \( A \) | Area parallel to force | \( \text{m}^2 \) |

### Shear Strain (Angular Deformation)

$$
\gamma = \frac{\Delta x}{h} = \tan\theta
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( \gamma \) | Shear strain | dimensionless |
| \( \Delta x \) | Lateral displacement | \( \text{m} \) |
| \( h \) | Height of material | \( \text{m} \) |
| \( \theta \) | Shear angle | \( \text{rad} \) or \( \degree \) |

### Shear Modulus (Rotational Cohesion)

$$
\tau = G \gamma
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( G \) | Shear modulus (modulus of rigidity) | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( \tau \) | Shear stress | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( \gamma \) | Shear strain | dimensionless |

### Relationship to Young's Modulus

$$
G = \frac{E}{2(1 + \nu)}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( E \) | Young's modulus | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( \nu \) | Poisson's ratio | dimensionless |

### Shear Force in Beams (Internal Distribution)

$$
V = \frac{dM}{dx}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( V \) | Shear force | \( \text{N} \) |
| \( M \) | Bending moment | \( \text{N} \cdot \text{m} \) |
| \( x \) | Position along beam | \( \text{m} \) |

### Shear Energy (Stored Cohesion)

$$
U = \frac{1}{2}G \gamma^2 V_{\text{ol}}
$$

Where \( V_{\text{ol}} \) is volume. Shear deformation stores elastic energy through rotational distortion.

## Key Properties of Shear Force

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Rotational** | Layers slide and rotate past one another |
| **Distortive** | Changes shape without changing volume |
| **Cohesive** | Binds layers into a coherent sheared structure |
| **Angular** | Produces angular deformation \( \gamma \) |
| **Material-dependent** | \( G \) varies widely (steel ~80 GPa, rubber ~0.001 GPa) |
| **Temperature-dependent** | Higher \( T \) → lower \( G \) (generally) |
| **Volume-preserving** | No change in volume under pure shear |

### Shear Strength (Failure Criterion)

$$
\tau_{\text{max}} = \frac{F_{\text{fail}}}{A}
$$

Where \( \tau_{\text{max}} \) is the maximum shear stress before failure. Ductile materials fail by shearing; brittle materials fail by tension.

### Mohr's Circle (Stress Transformation)

$$
\tau_{\text{max}} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_2}{2}
$$

Where \( \sigma_1 \) and \( \sigma_2 \) are principal stresses. Maximum shear occurs at 45° to principal axes.

### Shear Wave Propagation (Rotational Cohesion in Motion)

$$
v_s = \sqrt{\frac{G}{\rho}}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( v_s \) | Shear wave velocity | \( \text{m}/\text{s} \) |
| \( G \) | Shear modulus | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( \rho \) | Density | \( \text{kg}/\text{m}^3 \) |

## Real-Life Examples

| Example | How Shear Force Works |
|---|---|
| **1. Scissors cutting paper** | Blades apply opposing shear forces, sliding layers apart |
| **2. Punching a hole** | Shear stress exceeds material strength, cutting through |
| **3. Bolts in a joint** | Bolts resist shear as plates try to slide past each other |
| **4. Earthquake fault slip** | Shear stress releases suddenly, causing seismic displacement |
| **5. Metal cutting** | Cutting tools shear metal along a plane |
| **6. Playing cards** | Stacked cards slide and rotate under shear |
| **7. Guacamole mixing** | Spatula applies shear, distorting avocado layers |
| **8. Wind on a building** | Horizontal wind creates shear stress on structural frames |
| **9. Rivets in aircraft** | Rivets resist shear between fuselage panels |
| **10. Seismic S-waves** | Shear waves propagate through Earth's interior |

## Summary

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is shear force? | Rotational sliding force causing angular distortion between layers |
| What law describes it? | **Shear Modulus**: \( \tau = G \gamma \); **Beam Shear**: \( V = \frac{dM}{dx} \) |
| What type of force? | **Mid Cohesion** (rotational, Rule 2 + 4) |
| What drives it? | Tangential forces on parallel layers; angular distortion |
| Real examples? | Scissors, bolts, earthquakes, metal cutting, wind on buildings |
| What's its displacement counterpart? | **Tensile/Compression** (Mid Displacement) |



**Bottom line:** Shear force is the **rotational, angular-distorting slide** of parallel layers — the Mid Cohesion force that pairs with tensile/compression's linear axial push-pull in Superphysics' Solid Relationalities.
