These are the reaction classes where intermolecular forces (IMFs) play a **routine, mechanistically significant role**—not just as background solvent effects, but as active determinants of reactivity, selectivity, or assembly.


1. Nucleophilic Substitution (Micro Displacement)

<!-- - **IMFs involved:** Hydrogen bonding, ion–dipole, electrostatic

## Why These Six?

- They span **aqueous, organic, biological, and materials chemistry**.
- They are **routinely taught** and **industrially/biologically pervasive**.
- In each, IMFs are **not incidental**—they determine mechanism, rate, selectivity, or assembly.
- Together they cover the major contexts where noncovalent interactions are mechanistically decisive: **proton transfer (1), substitution (2), addition (3), recognition/assembly (4), bond cleavage by solvent (5), and solid formation (6)**.

**Note:** Redox reactions, radical reactions, pericyclic reactions, and polymerizations also involve IMFs (solvent effects, pre-association), but the six above are the classes where IMFs are most commonly and centrally invoked in standard chemistry curricula and practice. -->
